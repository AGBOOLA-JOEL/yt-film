# Opening hooks: never the same twice in a row

A channel whose films all open the same way looks templated, and look-alike videos can read as repetitive content.
**Every film picks a hook type different from the user's last three films** (ask, or keep a log in the project
notes). Avoid the worn bridge line "to understand it, we have to go back to…": let the story simply move on.

## The menu
1. **In your hand**: second person, present tense, an everyday object the story lives in ("Strike a match.").
2. **Number punch**: one shocking number, alone ("Fifty seconds.").
3. **The ending first**: state the outcome flatly, then ask how it could happen.
4. **The myth to bust**: "You've probably heard… It's not true. The real story is stranger."
5. **The quote**: one real line someone said (sourced), then who said it and why it matters.
6. **The document**: an object on screen being read: a letter, a telegram, a police note, typed as we watch
   (the Chadwick example: the forged note is typed as the lawyer reads it).
7. **The question**: a riddle the film answers ("How do you rob a bank that trusts you completely?").
8. **Two pictures**: a before and after side by side, one line each.
9. **The witness**: start from a minor person who saw it (the waitress, the guard, the housekeeper).
10. **The rule**: the law, rule or system someone broke, stated plainly, then who broke it.
11. **The price tag**: what it cost, in something the viewer can feel (a house, a year's wages, a country's budget).
12. **Dated scene**: a place and date in the middle of the action ("Cleveland, December 1904.").

Whichever type: open on the strangest true thing within ~10 seconds, and make the promise of the film (what the viewer
will find out) clear by ~30 seconds. The engine helps: comic pages, a document typing itself, a stamp, a counter.

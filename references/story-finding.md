# Finding the next story (any niche)

Use when the user asks "what should we make next", "find stories", "suggest videos", or gives a theme and wants
options. The engine suits **narrated, story-driven explainers in any niche**: history, true crime, business and
finance, science and invention, biographies, sport, mysteries, art and culture, travel and places, war, internet
history, even fiction. Ask the user their niche and audience if you don't know them; don't assume one.

## What makes a story work for this format
1. **A clear centre**: one person, one object, one company or one event, not a broad topic.
2. **A premise people barely believe**, in one sentence, that is **documented** (books, court records, major press,
   papers). Fiction is fine if the user says so; facts must still be right when it's real.
3. **A single decision or turning point** the story grows from, with the world moving around it.
4. **A turn at the end** that makes viewers say "wait, really?".
5. **Changing places and times** that give ~150 different images (cities, interiors, decades, documents, objects).
6. **Enough material** for a full, unhurried telling: a beginning, an escalation, a fall, an after. No length target:
   the story sets the length.

## The gap check (why this video, now)
For each candidate, search YouTube for the story in the user's language: how many videos, how long, how good, how
many views, how recent. The best candidates have **interest** (books, articles, a podcast, a foreign-language
documentary) but **no strong video yet** in the user's language. Note the top existing videos so the pitch can say
what this film would do better or differently.

## Where to dig
Long-form journalism archives, court records, old newspapers (Chronicling America, Trove, British Newspaper Archive),
Wikipedia's obscure categories, books' reading lists, history and mystery communities, museum blogs, podcasts in
other languages, Wikimedia Commons (for real photos the film can show).

## How to pitch (3–5 candidates)
For each: a working title, the one-sentence premise, the hook (hooks.md), why it fits the format (centre, turn,
places), the gap check result (with the best existing video and its views), what's documented and where, the risks
(thin sources, sensitive content), and roughly how many images it needs. End with a recommendation.

## Keep a list
Keep the user's done list and backlog in their own notes (not in this skill), so later pitches don't repeat them.

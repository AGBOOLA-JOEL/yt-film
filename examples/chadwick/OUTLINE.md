# Cassie Chadwick: "Carnegie's daughter" · facts and sources

Working title: *She Pretended to Be Carnegie's Daughter (And Banks Paid Her Millions)* — the one English YouTube
video on it has 511 views, so the gap is open.

**One person, one decision**: a deaf-ish farm girl from Ontario who forged her first letter at 13 decides, at about
45, to tell one lawyer one lie — that she is the secret daughter of the richest man in America — and lets him spread
it for her. Banks lend her millions for eight years on a sealed envelope nobody opens.
**Twist**: Carnegie never met her; the envelope held crude forgeries; and the man whose name she stole came to watch
her trial (reportedly) and later paid for a building in the ruined town.

## Hook (type: **the document**)
Hook choice: this one opens on
**a document being read**: a folded paper falls on a Fifth Avenue pavement; a lawyer picks it up; I type it live:
*"…promise to pay to the order of Cassie L. Chadwick… two million dollars… Andrew Carnegie."* Then: *"It was a
forgery. The woman who dropped it on purpose had been forging things since she was thirteen."* No "go back" line.

## Fact list (✅ two or more good sources · ⚠️ one source / later account, word as "reportedly" · ❌ conflicting)

**Ontario, 1857–1879**
- ✅ Born Elizabeth "Betsy" Bigley, 10 Oct 1857, Ontario (Eastwood per Cleveland encyclopedia; Appin / Strathroy in
  others — say "a small town in Ontario"). Father Daniel Bigley, railway section hand. [WP, ECH, EBSCO]
- ⚠️ A quiet child, partly deaf, given to long silences (Abbott, *Smithsonian* 2012). Say "people later said".
- ⚠️ First con at 13 (1870): a forged letter saying an uncle had died and left her money. [Abbott / Longform summary]
- ✅ 1879, Woodstock, Ontario: arrested for forged notes; calling card "Miss Bigley, Heiress to $15,000"; pleaded
  insanity, found not guilty (22 Mar 1879; age 21). [WP; ECH says "at 22"] ⚠️ made faces at the jury [EBSCO].
- ⚠️ Toronto: posed as "Elizabeth Cunard" of the shipping family with a forged letter, ~$10,000 of goods. [WP only]

**Cleveland and the names, 1880–1893**
- ✅ Sent to her married sister Alice York in Cleveland; took a loan on her sister's furniture. [WP]
- ✅ Fortune teller / clairvoyant as "Madame Lydia DeVere" (also "Lydia Scott"). [WP, ECH, EBSCO]
- ✅ 21 Nov 1882 married Dr Wallace S. Springsteen; he threw her out after 11–12 days when the *Plain Dealer*
  exposed her. [WP 12, ECH 11 → say "less than two weeks"]
- ⚠️ "Madame Marie LaRose", married farmer John R. Scott; divorce. [WP]
- ⚠️ "Mrs Hoover": C. L. Hoover, died 1888, ~$50,000 claimed; son Emil born c.1887. [WP]
- ✅ 1889 forgery conviction in Toledo (forged the name of Richard Brown of Youngstown, per EBSCO); 9½ years in the
  Ohio Penitentiary. [WP, ECH, EBSCO]
- ✅ Paroled c.1893 (ECH: by Governor William McKinley — the future president). Nice footnote.

**Dr Chadwick, 1893–1897**
- ✅ Back in Cleveland as "Mrs Cassie Hoover", ran a brothel; Dr Leroy S. Chadwick, a widowed physician, met her
  there. [WP, Encyclopedia.com] ⚠️ She told him she was only teaching the girls manners [Enc.com]; ⚠️ she fainted.
- ✅ Married Leroy Chadwick 1897; moved into his house on Euclid Avenue, "Millionaires' Row". [WP, ECH]
- ✅ Society shut her out. [WP]

**The lie, c.1902** (⚠️ the whole scene comes from later newspaper/book retellings; details vary)
- New York, Holland House hotel: she "runs into" Dillon, a Cleveland lawyer who knew Leroy; asks him to ride with her
  to Andrew Carnegie's Fifth Avenue house. He waits in the carriage; she is inside 20–30 minutes (EBSCO 20, Enc.com
  30) — inside she only asked the housekeeper about a maid's references. Coming out she waves at the door, trips on
  the carriage step, drops a paper: a promissory note for **$2 million** signed "Andrew Carnegie". She swears him
  to secrecy: she is Carnegie's illegitimate daughter; there are $7 million more in notes at home; she'll inherit
  ~$400 million. [WP, Enc.com, EBSCO]
- ❌ Year and house: accounts say 1902; Carnegie moved into his new 91st St mansion late 1902. Show "a grand Fifth
  Avenue house", never name the street in narration.
- ✅ Dillon arranged for the papers to go into the **Wade Park Bank**, Cleveland, in a **sealed envelope**; nobody
  opened it. [WP, EBSCO, 1904 papers] **Iri Reynolds**, secretary-treasurer, held it ("Iri, not Ira") and lent her
  much of his own money. [finhist / 1904 papers ⚠️]

**Queen of Ohio, 1902–1904**
- ✅ Loans of about $2 million in all (estimates $633k → $20M; say "millions"), often at very high interest and
  with bonuses to the bankers. [WP, EBSCO, Enc.com]
- ✅ Citizens National Bank of **Oberlin**: president **Charles T. Beckwith**, cashier **A. B. Spear**; she drained it
  (figures vary $200k–$800k) and took >$100,000 of Beckwith's own money. [EBSCO, WP, 1904 papers]
- ✅ Diamonds, 30 closets of clothes, a gold organ; "the Queen of Ohio". ⚠️ $100,000 dinner party [Enc.com].
- ✅ Nicknames in the papers: "High Priestess of Fraudulent Finance", "Queen of Swindlers", "Duchess of Diamonds".

**Collapse, Nov–Dec 1904**
- ✅ Herbert B. Newton, Brookline (Mass.) banker, lent $190,800; called it in; sued 22 Nov 1904 (ECH: "2 Nov"
  ❌ — say "November 1904"). [WP, ECH]
- ✅ Carnegie's office: he did not know her; he "had not signed a note in more than thirty years". [WP, Enc.com]
- ✅ Run on the Oberlin bank; it closed. Run on Wade Park. Envelope opened: forgeries. [WP, finhist, 1904 papers]
- ✅ She fled to New York; arrested 7 Dec 1904 at the Hotel Breslin, in bed, wearing a money belt with ~$100,000.
  [WP, Enc.com] Leroy left for Europe and divorced her. [WP]

**Trial and after, 1905–1907**
- ✅ Trial in Cleveland, March 1905; convicted 10 Mar 1905 on seven counts of conspiracy (against the US / to wreck
  the Oberlin bank); 14 years and a $70,000 fine. [ECH, WP, EBSCO] ⚠️ Carnegie in the courtroom [WP, EBSCO, others].
- ⚠️ Prosecutor: she had robbed widows and orphans "of their hopes of peace and prosperity in their old age". [EBSCO]
- ✅ Beckwith died before his trial; Spear got 7 years. [EBSCO, others]
- ✅ Entered the Ohio Penitentiary, Columbus, Jan 1906, with trunks of her furniture and clothes. [WP, ECH]
- ✅ Went blind Sept 1907; died 10 Oct 1907 — her 50th birthday; buried in Woodstock, Ontario. [WP]
- ⚠️ Carnegie later paid for a building at Oberlin / covered losses [WP, EBSCO] — word carefully.
- ✅ The Euclid Avenue house became a sight for tourists; demolished in the 1920s. [WP]

## Footnotes (5)
1. The carriage story is the lawyer's account, retold in the papers — we only have his word.
2. Her calling card: "Miss Bigley, Heiress to $15,000" (Woodstock, 1879).
3. The man who paroled her in 1893 was Governor William McKinley.
4. "Iri", not "Ira" — the banker who guarded the envelope.
5. She died on her 50th birthday.
